Qualifications & background

Technical support for counsel

A focused assessment of the technology and evidence.

Dr. Yoder brings more than 30 years of experience in academic research, industrial R&D, and scientific consulting to semiconductor technical questions.

His work connects semiconductor physics with the operation and performance of electronic and photonic devices. This background supports review of device documentation, scientific literature, physical mechanisms, and numerical simulations.

Potential engagements include patent disputes and other matters involving semiconductor technology. The initial discussion establishes the technical fit, scope, and availability.

Examples of potential assignments

Questions that turn on the device physics.

Does the proposed mechanism make physical sense?

Evaluate whether a described operating mechanism is consistent with carrier transport, electrostatics, material properties, and the disclosed device structure.

Does the simulation support the conclusion?

Examine model assumptions, material parameters, boundary conditions, numerical methods, and calibration against available measurements.

What do the measurements establish?

Assess the relationship between reported data and conclusions about gain, noise, bandwidth, saturation, transport, or breakdown.

How does the disclosed structure operate?

Explain how device geometry, doping, heterostructures, and material choices relate to the physical behavior described in technical documents or published literature.

Relevant subject matter

Specialist expertise in semiconductor devices and modeling.

Device simulation and carrier transport

Technology computer-aided design (TCAD), full-band Monte Carlo methods, quantum transport, first-principles modeling, and coupled electrothermal analysis.

Photodetectors and avalanche devices

Avalanche photodiodes (APDs), single-photon avalanche diodes (SPADs), impact ionization, gain and noise, ultraviolet detection, bandwidth, and photodiode nonlinearity.

III-nitrides and wide-bandgap materials

GaN, AlGaN, InGaN, and AlN; heterostructures, polarization, high-field transport, self-heating, doping, and impurity-band physics.

Photonics and emerging devices

Visible and ultraviolet lasers, vertical-cavity surface-emitting lasers, resonant tunneling structures, and millimeter-wave sources.
